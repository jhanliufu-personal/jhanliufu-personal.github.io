STEVO-Bench: Benchmarking Video World Models on State Evolution Beyond the Pixel Frame

  • Designed a benchmark to evaluate whether video world models can simulate world dynamics beyond visible pixels, probing understanding of physical, chemical, and social state evolution.
  • Introduced observation control mechanisms — occlusion, camera lookaway, illumination dimming — to reveal whether models track hidden state or rely purely on visual extrapolation.
  • Covers 225 tasks across 6 categories: scalar processes, few-object kinematics, structural/material transformations, intent-driven behavior, physical dynamics, and chemical/social world changes.

Project description

Modern video generation models can produce visually compelling sequences, but do they truly understand the world they depict? STEVO-Bench asks a harder question: can a video world model simulate state evolution that occurs beyond the pixel frame? When a reaction unfolds off-screen, when an object is occluded, or when the lights go out, the world keeps changing — a genuine world model should track that hidden state and reflect it when observation resumes.

The benchmark introduces observation control as a diagnostic tool. By selectively withholding visual information via occlusion, camera lookaway, and illumination dimming, we can distinguish models that reason about latent world state from those that merely hallucinate plausible-looking continuations. Each task presents a video segment followed by a controlled observation gap, then asks the model to predict what the world looks like when observation resumes.

The 225 tasks span six categories chosen to cover diverse axes of state evolution: scalar processes (gradual accumulation), few-object kinematics (simple dynamics), structural and material transformations (irreversible changes such as melting or burning), intent-driven behavior (agent actions with goals), and broader physical, chemical, and social world dynamics. The dataset contains 244 annotated examples hosted on Hugging Face, with evaluation code and a leaderboard on the project website.
