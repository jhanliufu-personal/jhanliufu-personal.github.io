Self-Organized Hot-Carrier–Based Artificial Leaf for Biointerfaces

Pengju Li, Mengzhan Liufu, Cooper R. Johnston, Young-Woo Pyo, Ananth Kamath, Ruipeng Li, Yuzi Liu ... Hong-Gyu Park, Sarah B.King, Bozhi Tian. *equal contributors

Journal: Nature Photonics (under review) (2025)

Abstract

Without discrete pixels or wired leads, natural plant leaves respond to light and relay electrochemical signals to surrounding tissues — an elegant capability sought in next-generation optoelectronic systems. Although semiconductors and their heterojunctions are commonly employed to mimic photosynthesis (e.g., in artificial leaves), plasmonic metal nanostructures offer a largely untapped alternative. Their compelling advantages in broadband absorption, tunable resonance, and strong local field enhancement are achievable within ultrathin, scalable architectures. However, efficiently harnessing the hot carriers generated by plasmons for use in macroscopic systems remains a significant challenge, hindering broader application in areas like tissue-level neuromodulation and human-machine interfaces. We present a hot-carrier artificial leaf metasurface—architected through thermal self-organization of discrete 3D gold–titanium dioxide plasmonic units onto ultrathin monolithic membranes. These 3D interfaces concentrate hot-carrier activity, with titanium dioxide scaffolds enhancing optical, optoelectronic, and stability properties. Moving beyond artificial leaves' traditional scope in solar fuel production, our metasurfaces enable leadless, multimodal biological tissue modulation with unprecedented bioelectrical polarity control. This control manifests spatially through on-demand conducting polymer patterning or temporally via switching of multimodal activation mechanisms. We further show AI-assisted, neurotechnology-inspired, pixel-less optical pattern recognition, marking the first known examples of contactless human-screen interface of its kind. This self-organized, hot-carrier–based artificial leaf platform holds promise for scalable and minimally invasive biomedical, neuromorphic, and extended reality applications.
